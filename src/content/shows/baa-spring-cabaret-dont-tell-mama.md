Don't Tell Mama is a classic Midtown cabaret room — intimate tables, a close audience, and a night built on short, high-stakes turns rather than a full book musical. Spring cabarets from training programs like Broadway Artists Alliance put young performers into that professional off-Broadway context: share a program with peers, hold a song or scene under club lighting, and treat the evening as a real booking rather than a classroom showing.

Elyse performed in the Broadway Artists Alliance spring cabaret at Don't Tell Mama. The credit sits alongside her earlier NYC cabaret work and her BAA training — proof that she can take Alliance preparation into a working cabaret venue, stay stage-present with a live band and a talkative room, and represent cleanly in a company that casting and industry guests often attend.

For readers scanning credits, this is New York cabaret experience in a named off-Broadway house, not only a studio showcase — useful context next to musical theatre roles and her Ripley-Grier solo work.
