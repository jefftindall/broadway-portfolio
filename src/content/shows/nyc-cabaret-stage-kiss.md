Cabaret and industry rooms demand a different kind of bravery than a full musical: no set to hide behind, no ensemble to share the load — only text, timing, and the ability to hold a room. *Stage Kiss* is Sarah Ruhl’s sharp comedy about actors, desire, and the blurry line between performance and real feeling. A monologue from that world needs wit, vulnerability, and precise listening to an imagined partner.

Elyse’s New York City cabaret debut at Ripley-Grier Studios featured her *Stage Kiss* monologue — a milestone in her off-Broadway and NYC training journey after Broadway Artists Alliance work. Solo cabaret is casting-relevant because it proves she can shape an arc alone, land comic and emotional turns without scenic support, and stay present for an audience that is often industry-adjacent and close enough to see every choice.

This credit pairs with her musical roles to show versatility: she can thrive inside large productions and also deliver intimate, text-forward work that reads cleanly on camera and in small venues.
