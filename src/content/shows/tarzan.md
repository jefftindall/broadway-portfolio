Disney’s *Tarzan* tells of a man raised by gorillas who must reconcile two worlds — jungle and human society — through Phil Collins’s pop-inflected score and movement-heavy storytelling. Many productions lean on athletic dance, animal characterization, and company numbers that feel kinetic rather than drawing-room polite. Featured dancers help define that physical language: they set the quality of the tribe, the intensity of the jungle, and the polish of transitions that keep the adventure moving.

Elyse was a featured dancer at The Grand Theater, contributing specialty movement and company strength in a musical that rewards stamina and bold physical choices. Featured dance tracks require picking up choreography quickly, maintaining character in motion, and staying safe while still reading “effortless” from the house. Choreographers notice who counts, who spots, and who can repeat the combination on the second try.

Alongside her Elsa and other acting credits at the same venue, *Tarzan* reinforces Elyse’s triple-threat profile: she is not only a storyteller who sings, but a mover directors can trust with featured dance responsibility.
