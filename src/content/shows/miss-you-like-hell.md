*Miss You Like Hell*, with book by Quiara Alegría Hudes and music and lyrics by Erin McKeown, traces a fraught mother-daughter road trip across America — deportation fears, online confession, and the messy work of reconnection. Olivia is the daughter at the story’s center: young, complicated, and vocally contemporary. The inaugural regional production at Woodstock Arts staged that journey with intimate lighting and a road marked across the floor — everyday costumes, high emotional stakes.

Elyse performed in the ensemble and understudied Olivia, the main character track. That dual assignment is demanding: ensemble work requires musical precision and storytelling in group numbers, while covering Olivia means owning the full emotional and vocal arc of a leading role — ready to go on with little notice. Onstage she brought the same grounded, contemporary presence the piece asks for: listening hard, staying in the relationship, and letting the road-trip intimacy read clearly in a close house.

For casting, this credit signals comfort with contemporary musical theatre, emotionally grounded material, and professional understudy discipline on a premiere — not a revival with decades of reference recordings alone.
